The problem

Three records. Three different stories.

When a paid order goes missing, each system only knows its own part. Support ends up stitching it together by hand while the customer waits.

  1. Your checkout page

    “Payment successful. Your ticket is on its way.”

    What the customer saw. Reassuring, but it doesn’t prove anything was delivered.

  2. PayPal

    Completed · USD 29.00

    The money moved. That settles the payment, not the delivery.

  3. Your store

    0 tickets for this order

    The customer has nothing. Now you know where to look.

CrashCart reads each record on its own terms, then checks them against your promise: one ticket, within 60 seconds of a confirmed payment.

Inside the workspace

The answer first, then the proof

Every case opens with whether the promise was kept. Below that are the three records behind the answer and the one thing worth doing next.

How it works

From “they paid” to “here’s why” in four steps

  1. 01

    Make a test purchase

    Start a checkout in PayPal’s test environment and approve it yourself. No real card is charged.

  2. 02

    Confirm the payment yourself

    After you approve it on PayPal, you confirm the payment with a click. CrashCart never completes a charge on its own.

  3. 03

    Check the delivery promise

    CrashCart asks PayPal and your store directly, then applies the same rule every time: one ticket, within 60 seconds.

  4. 04

    Let the assistant dig in

    If something’s off, an AI assistant works through the records in the background. It suggests where to look, but the payment and ticket records decide the result. You can stop it at any time.

Why you can trust the answer

Answers you can act on, and stand behind

  • The records decide, not the AI.

    The assistant suggests where to look. Whether the promise was kept comes from PayPal’s payment record and your store’s ticket record, checked against one fixed rule.

  • No double charge from a dropped reply.

    If PayPal’s reply never arrives, CrashCart reads the saved order before anyone tries again. A lost reply is never treated as a failed payment.

  • “Not sure yet” is an honest answer.

    When the records can’t prove what happened, you’ll see “More evidence needed” and what to check next, not a guess.

  • Every case keeps its receipts.

    Download a case’s full records to share with your team, or a replay test that reruns the failure on your computer with no new payment.

Questions

Good to know before you start

Does CrashCart take real money?

No. Test checkouts run in PayPal Sandbox, PayPal’s test environment. You approve each payment yourself on PayPal’s test site, then confirm it in CrashCart with a click. Nothing is charged or confirmed on its own.

Which stores does it work with today?

One owned workshop test store, selling a $29 ticket promised within 60 seconds of payment. Shopify and WooCommerce readers exist as a separate preview, but they haven’t been connected to a real store yet, so there’s no install or connect flow today.

What does the AI assistant actually do?

It reads a case’s saved payment and store records in the background, suggests where the delivery went wrong and explains what it found. It doesn’t decide the result, refund anyone, change an order or contact a customer. You can stop it at any time; stopping keeps the records and doesn’t reverse a payment.

What if PayPal’s reply gets lost?

CrashCart says it can’t tell yet, reads the saved order and checks with PayPal before you decide anything. It never retries a payment step automatically.

What on this page is real?

The animation, walkthrough and workspace picture are illustrations. They run in your browser and don’t create a case, take a payment or ask the AI anything. In your workspace, every result comes from that case’s own saved records, and each case is labelled as a new test checkout, a repair check, a saved replay or example data.

What does my workspace need?

An access key from your workspace owner, plus PayPal Sandbox details for test checkouts. The assistant needs model access with a usage allowance. The records explorer and delivery timing views are optional and need their own licensed setup. The workspace’s setup panel shows which details are saved. “Saved” means the details exist, not that the connection has been tested.

What can I take away from a case?

The case records as JSON, which you can read but which don’t run anything. When a confirmed payment was left without its ticket by a lost PayPal reply or a stopped store, you also get a replay test (ZIP) that reruns the failure on your computer with no new payment.
